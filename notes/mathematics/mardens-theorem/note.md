# Marden's Theorem

## Summary

For a cubic polynomial with three non-collinear complex roots, the two roots of the derivative are exactly the foci of the Steiner inellipse of the triangle formed by the roots.

## Key Ideas

- The roots of a cubic form a triangle in the complex plane.
- Vieta's formulas show the derivative roots have midpoint equal to the triangle centroid.
- The Steiner inellipse is tangent to the side midpoints and is centered at the centroid.
- Carlson's proof carefully uses a stretch to reduce the geometry to an equilateral triangle without pretending that arbitrary affine maps preserve foci.
- The logarithmic derivative gives an electrostatic balance interpretation.

## Important Equations

```tex
p'(z)=0
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
\text{the foci of the Steiner inellipse}
```
